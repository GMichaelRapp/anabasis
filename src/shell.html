                                                Your privacy

                                                Anabasis has no account, no server and no tracking. Your project lives in two places only: the .json file you save, and a working copy in this browser (IndexedDB) so nothing is lost if the tab closes. Anabasis connects to the internet only when you ask it to: looking up a source by DOI (doi.org, Crossref, DataCite), ISBN (Open Library) or web address (Wikipedia’s citation service), or backing up to GitHub. A locked project is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) before it is saved or backed up. Anything a project file contains is cleaned on opening, so a file someone sends you can’t run code or call out to the web.

                                                A project holds documents, notes and sources. It autosaves in this browser as you work, but the real copy is the .json file you save. Keep that file somewhere backed up (a synced folder or a private GitHub repo).

                                                Writing

                                                • “ Cite inserts a citation at the cursor. Pick one or more sources, add page or other locators, and it formats in the document’s style. Note styles (Chicago notes & bibliography) number it as a note. Author-date, APA and MLA show a parenthetical.
                                                • Click any citation or footnote marker to edit or remove it. Notes and the bibliography rebuild themselves below the text, with Ibid. and short forms handled automatically.
                                                • Switch the style from the menu next to the title. Every citation reformats at once.
                                                • Pasted text keeps basic formatting (headings, bold, italics, lists, links) and drops the rest. Use Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+V for plain text.

                                                Drafting

                                                • TK leaves a flag where something’s missing: a fact (TK), a citation (cite?), or something to verify (check). Typing the letters TK counts too. Everything to resolve is listed under Outline. Click an item to jump to it.
                                                • Outline also lists your headings with section word counts, and paragraphs of 40+ words with no citation. Tick highlight to mark those in the text.
                                                • → Note turns selected text into a note, linked to the source if the passage carries a citation. With nothing selected it opens a blank note for a quick idea.
                                                • Focus hides everything but the page, keeps your line centered and shows your word count and session progress. Set word targets in Page setup and a session goal in Settings.
                                                • Search (Ctrl/Cmd+K) finds anything in your documents, notes and sources, and runs commands. Ctrl/Cmd+F is find and replace in the current document.

                                                Notecards & the board

                                                • ▦ Board (Notes tab) lays your notes out as cards. Group them into piles you name (chapters, arguments, “maybe”), or by tag, source or kind. Or switch to free canvas and arrange them anywhere, like index cards on a table.
                                                • Drag cards to reorder them or move them between columns. Moving a card changes its pile, tag, source or kind to match the column. Board order is also the default order for Compile. Dragging works with a mouse, trackpad or touch.
                                                • Shift- or ⌘/Ctrl-click to select several cards, then color, pin, tag, move, merge, delete, or draft from these (a new document from the cards, in order).
                                                • In a note, Split breaks the card in two at the cursor. Write [[Another note]] to link notes. Links and backlinks show under the note, and clicking a link to a note that doesn’t exist offers to create it.
                                                • Drag a note from the Notes list straight into your draft to drop it where you let go.

                                                Getting sources in

                                                • Paste a URL, DOI or ISBN into the Sources tab. URLs are looked up through Wikipedia’s citation service (Citoid) for author, date and publication. If a site blocks it or you’re offline, the URL is saved and you fill in the rest.
                                                • ✂ Web clipper gives you a bookmark button that clips any page, plus a selected passage, straight into your project. It needs Anabasis at a web address (GitHub Pages works), not opened as a local file.
                                                • Keep Anabasis open in one tab. Clips sent from other pages go to that tab automatically. If you open a second tab, you’ll get a warning so the two don’t overwrite each other.

                                                Nonfiction tools

                                                • Quote check. Quotes inserted from quote notes stay linked to the note. If the wording in the draft drifts from your note, Outline → Quotes flags it. Compare shows the difference, and you can restore the original or update the note. Ellipses (…) and [bracketed changes] count as legitimate edits. Long quotations typed or pasted without a note are listed as unlinked.
                                                • Names & spellings. Keep a list of people, organizations, places and terms with their preferred spelling. Variants (Kiev, Prigozin) are highlighted in the draft and fixable in one click. Anabasis also suggests near-duplicate spellings it finds.
                                                • Source audit (Sources tab). Citations and share per source, a warning when one chapter leans on a single source, quoted words per source for permissions, sources missing details, and sources you haven’t used.
                                                • Timeline. Give notes an event date (when it happened), then set the board to Timeline to see them in order. Templates → Chronology turns them into a document.
                                                • Comments. Select text and click 💬. Comments are listed under Outline and travel to Word as real margin comments. They’re left out of PDF, ePub and other exports.
                                                • Revision lens. Highlights long sentences (35+ words), passive voice, hedges and filler, and repeated words, and shows grade level. It runs on your device, so nothing is sent anywhere.
                                                • Templates for front and back matter: dedication, epigraph, preface, acknowledgments, a note on sources, glossary, about the author. Also an annotated bibliography built from each source’s Annotation field.
                                                • Record. Words added per day, your streak, and each chapter’s status and deadline (also shown in the binder).
                                                • Transcripts. On a source, click Transcript…, paste a transcript, select lines, and make quote or paraphrase notes with the timestamp as the locator.
                                                • Versions. Save a named version, see what changed since (word-level), or restore it. Restoring saves your current text as a version first.
                                                • Import Word brings in .docx drafts with footnotes as text footnotes. Import references reads BibTeX and RIS as well as Zotero and OSINT lists. Export → ePub makes an e-book with tappable notes.

                                                Charts, maps, timelines & relationships

                                                • The figure button in the toolbar now offers an image, a chart from data (paste a table or use one from the document), a map, a timeline from your dated notes, or a relationship map. Each becomes a numbered figure with a caption and source line; click it later and choose Edit to change it.
                                                • Maps work offline: shade countries, search about 7,300 places (or save your own), place markers, and draw routes, arrows and hatched areas, with a legend you label. Borders come from Natural Earth, with Crimea shown as part of Ukraine; other disputed lines follow Natural Earth’s de facto view.
                                                • Timeline: notes with an event date line up on the board’s Timeline view (Group by: Timeline). Gaps in the record are marked, and Insert as figure turns them into a graphic. Dates can be years, “Aug 1680”, full dates, ranges like “1680–1692”, or BCE.
                                                • Relationships (Docs tab) maps the people, organizations and places in your Names list by how often they appear together, with the passages one click away. Shift-click two names to add your own labeled link.

                                                Checking the draft

                                                • Corroboration (Outline tab) lists paragraphs that rest on one source, or only on sources rated D–E or 4–5 in the Admiralty scale, and shows which sources carry the most citations. It warns when one source carries more than a third of them.
                                                • House style (Outline tab → Rules…) checks numbers, percent, US or U.S., the serial comma, date order, em dashes, curly quotes, ellipses, double spaces and your preferred spellings (Kyiv, not Kiev). Each rule has a Fix all button. Rules are saved with the project.
                                                • Accessibility (Outline tab) flags figures without alt text, headings that skip a level, tables without a header row, and links that say “here” or show a bare address. Exporting to HTML, ePub or Word reminds you if anything is left.
                                                • Find and replace in all documents (the find bar → All documents…, or the command palette) searches every document and, if you like, your notes. Tick the matches to change. Citations and footnotes are never touched, and each changed document gets a safety copy in Drafts first.

                                                Checkpoints, transcripts & promotion

                                                • Project checkpoints (Export menu) save a copy of the whole project. Compare one with now to see documents added, removed or changed, then open any changed document side by side in Drafts. Restoring saves the current state as a checkpoint first. Checkpoints live in this browser; Download keeps one elsewhere.
                                                • Transcripts: in the transcript window, Open file… reads caption files (.srt, .vtt), text, or Word. Caption fragments are joined into sentences with speakers and timestamps, and the search box filters the lines.
                                                • Promo kit (Export menu) drafts a numbered thread for X, Bluesky, Threads, Mastodon or LinkedIn with a counter on every post, a Substack subtitle and search description, and quote cards (square, wide or portrait) as PNG.
                                                • Maps can show oblast and province borders (Ukraine, Russia, Belarus, the Middle East, China, the Koreas, Japan, Taiwan, South Asia, the US and neighbors), rivers and lakes with names, a scale bar, and a locator inset when zoomed in. With provinces on, Shade colors a single province. Charts can mark events and shaded periods, fill areas, or split into small multiples on a shared scale.

                                                Acronyms, estimative language & scripts

                                                • Acronyms (Outline tab) are learned when you spell them out and flagged when undefined, defined late or twice, or used once. One click fixes each. A List of Abbreviations fills in on export.
                                                • Estimative language (Outline tab; on automatically for briefs and white papers) underlines vague words like “may” or “could” and offers terms from the ICD 203 likelihood scale. It also flags mixed wordings of the scale, confidence and likelihood in one sentence, and Key Judgments with no confidence level.
                                                • Script mode (Page setup → “This is a video script”) shows run time at your speaking rate, timecodes beside each paragraph or A/V row, two-column A/V tables, “Copy narration”, and a teleprompter. Space plays and pauses, ⌘/Ctrl+↑↓ changes speed, and Mirror flips the text for a prompter mirror.
                                                • GitHub backup (Export menu) commits the project file to a private repository on demand or whenever you save, and can reopen any earlier backup.

                                                Drafts

                                                • Drafts are made only when you choose. Click Drafts in the bar above the page (or press ⌘/Ctrl+Alt+D) to save one: a fixed copy of this document as it is now, numbered Draft 1, 2, 3…, with an optional name and a note such as “Sent to editor”.
                                                • Compare side by side: the Drafts view lines up any two drafts, or a draft and your current text, paragraph by paragraph. Rewritten paragraphs show the exact words cut and added; new, cut and moved paragraphs are marked down the left edge. Unchanged stretches fold away. J and K jump between changes. You can also compare against another document, such as an alternate version.
                                                • Bring things back: when the right side is your current text, Use this version, Put it back and Remove it work paragraph by paragraph. Restore brings back a whole draft, and Copy to new doc keeps a draft as its own document. Before Anabasis replaces any text for you, it keeps a safety copy, listed separately from your drafts.
                                                • Download comparison saves the side-by-side view as a page you can send to an editor or print.

                                                Figures, tables & the margin

                                                • Figures: the picture button inserts an image, chart or map; you can also paste a screenshot or drop image files onto the page. Type the caption underneath. Figures number themselves (Figure 1, 2…). Click an image to resize it, add alt text, or replace it. Large images are scaled down to 1800 px, and they travel inside the project file.
                                                • Tables: the grid button inserts one with a caption above. Tab moves between cells and adds a row at the end; the bar above the table adds and removes rows and columns. Word import now keeps images and tables.
                                                • List of figures & tables: add it from Templates (front matter), the ⌘K command search, or the manuscript compile options. It fills in on export, with page numbers in PDF.
                                                • Marginalia: ✎ in the toolbar (⌘/Ctrl+Alt+A) writes a note in the margin beside the line, anchored to the cursor or to selected text. Esc goes back to the text, and an empty note disappears. On narrow screens notes fold into numbered tabs. Page setup decides what exports do with them: Word comments, a separate Marginalia page at the end (with page numbers in PDF and Word), or leave them out.

                                                Substack, imports, autosave & locking

                                                • Export → Substack post copies the document ready to paste: notes become a numbered Notes section, figures stay images, and tables are drawn as images because Substack has no tables. The .zip package has the post and every image, in case something doesn’t paste.
                                                • Import .md (Notes tab) takes a whole folder, such as Notecards or an Obsidian vault, or a few files. Subfolders can become board piles.
                                                • Autosave to file (Settings, Chrome and Edge): after you save the project once, Anabasis writes to the same .json a few seconds after you stop typing. After a restart the browser may ask for one click (Resume autosave) before it can write again.
                                                • Lock project (Export menu) encrypts the project file and this browser’s copy with a password (AES-256, PBKDF2). There’s no recovery if you forget it. PDFs opened in the reader aren’t locked.

New project… starts from a kind (book, article, Substack series, white paper, intelligence brief, video script, literary journalism) with a plan template, citation style and target preset.                                                 Planning

                                                • ▤ Plan (Docs tab) is an outliner for structure before drafting. Enter adds a line, Tab and Shift+Tab indent and outdent, Alt+↑/↓ moves an item, ⌘/Ctrl+. folds it, and ⠿ drags it (drop on the right side to nest). Each item has a synopsis, a word target, a status, and linked notes, sources and document. Drag notes from the Notes list onto an item to link them.
                                                • Items can be sections, claims, evidence, counterpoints, research questions or ideas. Argument check lists claims without evidence, evidence without a source, unanswered counterpoints, open questions, and sections running short of their targets.
                                                • Index cards shows one level as cards with synopses. Drag to reorder, and double-click to go inside. Focus branch narrows the plan to one chapter.
                                                • Templates: argument essay, policy brief / white paper, intelligence brief, op-ed, narrative journalism, explainer / video script, Substack article series, nonfiction book, book proposal.
                                                • Build drafts turns the plan into documents with headings, synopsis placeholders (as TK flags) and linked notes. The plan then tracks drafted words against targets.
                                                • Freewrite: a timed sprint (optionally with no deleting) saved as a note and linked to the plan item it was about.
                                                • In the Outline tab, drag ⠿ to move whole sections (or single paragraphs, with paragraphs ticked, which gives a reverse outline). ← and → promote and demote headings. Notes renumber automatically, and Undo move reverses the last change.

If the PDF’s page numbers differ from the printed ones, set the page offset (PDF page 15 = printed 1 → 14). PDFs with built-in page labels are handled automatically.

That uses the PDF’s DOI when it has one, otherwise its title, author and date. Linked PDFs are kept in this browser so they reopen from the source, but they’re never put in your project file.

                                                OSINT Style Guide citations

                                                • Choose OSINT Style Guide v1.5 in a document’s style menu. Citations become footnotes in the guide’s form, built by the OSINT Citation Generator’s own formatter: full form the first time, short form after. There is no bibliography, as the guide specifies.
                                                • Open any source and expand OSINT Style Guide details to set the description (the full 771-term library), details, qualifiers, access notes, rating, language, archive copy and commentary. The preview shows the finished footnote and the generator’s fix/check warnings.
                                                • Anything you leave blank is filled from the source’s regular fields: authors, title, container as location, date, URL/DOI. Journal volume/issue and page ranges are filled the same way.
                                                • Type See, Cited in or Quoted in as a citation’s prefix to open the footnote with it. The suffix becomes commentary after the citation.
                                                • Import sources… opens list files saved by the OSINT Citation Generator, and Export → Sources as OSINT Citation Generator list writes one back, so you can audit a list there.

                                                Notes

                                                • Notes are written in Markdown and can be tagged, typed (quote, paraphrase, summary, idea) and linked to a source and page.
                                                • Insert at cursor drops a note into the current document with its citation. Quotes of 100+ words become block quotes, shorter ones get quotation marks.
                                                • Compile notes… turns a filtered, ordered set of notes into a new draft, optionally grouped by tag or by source.
                                                • Import .md reads Markdown files, such as Notecards notes. The first # heading becomes the title. tags: front matter or #hashtags become tags.

                                                Sources

                                                • Enter details by hand, or quick-add by DOI (via doi.org / Crossref) or ISBN (via Open Library). Lookups need an internet connection. Everything else works offline.
                                                • Type titles the way they should print. CSL styles can’t reliably convert title case to APA’s sentence case.
                                                • Zotero users: export a collection as CSL JSON and import it here. Sources export back out the same way.
