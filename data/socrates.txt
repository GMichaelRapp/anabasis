# Socrates: question stems for researchers working alone.
# "@ id | Group name | suits" starts a group; "suits" lists the selections it fits best
# (term, claim, number, source, person, event, none). Each following line is one stem.
# ___ is a blank: the first is filled with the selection, any second one is left for the writer.

@ clarify | Clarifying meaning and terms | term event none
What exactly do I mean by ___?
How would I explain ___ to someone who has never heard of it, in two sentences?
What is the difference between ___ and ___?
Is ___ one thing, or several things sharing a name?
Where does ___ begin and end? What is just outside it?
What would count as an example of ___? What looks like one but isn’t?
What is the strongest example of ___? The weakest that still counts?
How did the people at the time use the word ___? How do I use it?
Has the meaning of ___ changed since my sources were written?
Who coined ___, and what did they want it to do?
Is ___ a description, a judgment, or both?
What would I lose if I stopped using the word ___ and described the thing plainly?
What does ___ mean in this field that it doesn’t mean in ordinary speech?
Am I using ___ the same way in every chapter?
What is the opposite of ___? Is there one?
What scale is ___ on: a person, a unit, a city, a state, a region, an era?
When I say ___ “caused”, “shaped” or “drove” ___, what do I actually claim happened?
Which of my terms would a hostile reviewer ask me to define first?
What is the point of this paragraph, in one sentence?
What question is ___ an answer to?

@ evidence | Evidence and how we know | claim number source none
How do I know ___?
What is the single best piece of evidence for ___?
What is the best evidence against ___?
Which source, exactly, says ___? Have I read it myself, or only someone quoting it?
Is ___ something a source saw, heard about, worked out, or assumed?
How many independent sources support ___? Do they all trace back to one?
What evidence would I expect to find if ___ were true? Have I looked for it?
What evidence would I expect to find if ___ were false? Have I looked for that?
What evidence is missing that should exist? Why might it be missing?
Is the absence of evidence for ___ meaningful here, or just a gap in the record?
Would this evidence convince someone who starts out doubting ___?
Am I quoting ___ in a way the author would recognise?
Does the evidence support the claim as I’ve worded it, or a weaker one?
Which of my claims rests on a single source?
What is the date of the evidence, and does it fit the date of the event?
Is this evidence direct, or am I inferring ___ from it?
Could the same evidence support a different conclusion?
How much of ___ is documented, and how much is my reconstruction?
What would it take to change my mind about ___?
Where is the original: the document, the dataset, the recording, the photograph?
Has anyone tried to check ___ before me? What did they find?
Is this a fact, a widely repeated claim, or a figure that has hardened by being repeated?
If my best source on ___ turned out to be wrong, what would still stand?

@ assumptions | Assumptions | claim event none
What has to be true for ___ to hold?
What am I taking for granted about ___?
Which of my assumptions, if wrong, would bring down the whole argument?
Which assumption have I never tested because it seems obvious?
Am I assuming ___ acted rationally? Rational by whose lights?
Am I assuming ___ knew what I know now?
Am I assuming the people involved wanted what they said they wanted?
Am I assuming the official account of ___ is the accurate one? Or that it is a lie?
Am I assuming that what was planned is what happened?
Am I assuming the record is complete?
Am I assuming ___ was unusual? Compared with what?
Am I assuming ___ will continue, because it has so far?
Am I assuming a single actor where there were many, or many where there was one?
What does my field assume about ___ that a neighbouring field would question?
What assumptions did my best source bring to ___?
What would someone from ___ (another country, era, party, profession) find strange in my framing?
Which assumptions have I inherited from the first book I read on this?
If I listed my assumptions in order of confidence, which is at the bottom?
What would change if I assumed the opposite of ___ for a page?
What assumption am I making about my reader?

@ alternatives | Alternatives and competing explanations | claim event none
What else could explain ___?
What is the most boring explanation for ___?
What is the explanation my strongest critic would give?
If I had to argue the opposite of ___, what would I say first?
Could ___ be coincidence? How would I tell?
Could ___ be the result of incompetence rather than design? Or design rather than incompetence?
Could the cause run the other way: does ___ cause ___?
Could a third thing explain both ___ and ___?
Which explanation fits all the evidence, not just the vivid pieces?
Which piece of evidence fits my explanation badly? What explains it better?
What evidence would tell these explanations apart?
Which explanations have I dismissed without testing? Why?
How have other writers explained ___? Where do they disagree, and why?
What would someone who was there say happened?
What explanation would have seemed obvious at the time?
Is there a structural explanation (money, geography, institutions) for what I’ve put down to individuals?
Is there an individual explanation for what I’ve put down to structures?
If two explanations are both partly true, how much does each explain?
What explanation would I be embarrassed to have missed?

@ implications | Implications and consequences | claim event none
If ___ is right, what else must be true?
If ___ is right, what should I expect to find in sources I haven’t read yet?
What follows from ___ that I haven’t said out loud?
Who would be affected if ___ were true? How?
What would happen next if ___ continued?
What are the second-order effects of ___, the consequences of the consequences?
What did ___ make possible that wasn’t possible before? What did it rule out?
Who gained from ___, and who paid for it?
What were the unintended consequences of ___?
If ___ is true, does it contradict anything I wrote in an earlier chapter?
Does ___ hold only in this case, or does it generalise? How far?
What would change in how people act if they believed ___?
What does ___ mean for policy, practice or the next decision someone faces?
What prediction does my argument make that could be checked later?
If I’m wrong about ___, what is the cost, and who bears it?
So what? Why should anyone care that ___?

@ perspectives | Perspectives, actors and stakeholders | person event none
How would ___ describe what happened?
What did ___ want? What did they fear?
What did ___ know at the time, and what couldn’t they know?
What options did ___ actually have? Which did they think they had?
Whose voice is missing from my account of ___?
Who appears in my sources only as a number, a crowd or a victim?
Who kept the records, and who was recorded?
How did ___ look from the other side: the adversary, the target, the rival?
How did ordinary people experience ___, as opposed to leaders?
How did ___ look from the capital, and how from the provinces or the front?
What would a participant object to in my account?
Who would be angry to read this, and would they have a point?
Who had the power to decide ___, and who only had to live with it?
Which actor am I treating as a single mind when it was a committee, a faction or a bureaucracy?
Within ___, who disagreed, and who won the argument?
What did ___ say in public, and what in private?
How have descendants, successors or later governments remembered ___?
Whose interests does the standard story of ___ serve?
If I swapped the names of the two sides, would I judge the actions the same way?

@ sources | Sources and provenance | source none
Who made ___, when, where, and for whom?
Why was ___ written, recorded or kept? What was it for?
Was the author of ___ in a position to know? First-hand, second-hand, or further?
How long after the event was ___ written? What happened in between?
What did the author of ___ stand to gain or lose?
Who was the intended audience, and how might that have shaped what was said?
What does ___ leave out that its author would certainly have known?
Is ___ the original, a copy, a translation, an excerpt or a summary? Who made the change?
Has ___ been edited, redacted, censored or selectively released? By whom?
How did ___ survive when other records didn’t?
Can ___ be authenticated? What would a forgery or a fake look like here?
What else did this author write, and how reliable has it proved?
Does ___ agree with sources that had no contact with it?
Where ___ and ___ disagree, which is more likely right, and why?
Is this source primary, secondary or tertiary for the claim I’m using it for?
Is the figure I’m citing the source’s own, or one it borrowed?
What is the earliest appearance of ___ that I can find?
How has this source been read by others? Am I reading it against the grain or with it?
If ___ is a web page, has it changed since I read it? Is there an archived copy?
If ___ is an image or video, where and when was it really taken?
What would the source’s critics say about its reliability?
How much weight can this source bear: colour, corroboration, or the whole claim?

@ causation | Causation | event claim
Why did ___ happen when it did, and not earlier or later?
Why did ___ happen here, and not somewhere else that looked similar?
What were the long-term conditions, the medium-term pressures and the trigger for ___?
Was ___ necessary for ___ to happen? Was it enough on its own?
What would have had to be different for ___ not to happen?
Which causes were deliberate choices, and which were circumstances?
Which cause matters most? How would I measure that?
Am I mistaking the sequence of events for a chain of causes?
Am I mistaking a correlation between ___ and ___ for cause and effect?
What is the mechanism: how, step by step, did ___ produce ___?
Who could have stopped ___, and why didn’t they?
What role did chance, weather, illness, timing or error play?
What role did ideas, beliefs or misperceptions play, beyond interests?
Were there feedback loops, where ___ made itself more likely?
Did ___ have one cause, or is the honest answer “several, tangled”?
Is my explanation the cause of ___, or the justification people gave for it?
Would the same causes produce the same result elsewhere? Have they?

@ time | Time, chronology and change | event term
What happened before ___, and what came right after?
Am I sure of the order? Which dates are firm, and which are estimates?
What changed, and what stayed the same, between ___ and ___?
When did ___ start? Is that when it became visible, or when it began?
When did people realise ___ was happening? How long after it began?
Was the change sudden, gradual, or sudden after a long build-up?
What was the turning point, and was it seen as one at the time?
Which was faster, the event or the reaction to it?
Am I reading later knowledge back into ___?
How did ___ look a week, a year and a decade later?
What else was happening at the same time that people were paying attention to?
Which timescale explains ___ best: hours, years, generations, centuries?
What does the timeline look like if I put the evidence in date order, not the order I found it?
Is there a gap in the timeline? What happened in it?
Was ___ the beginning of something, the end of something, or the middle?
Is the story of decline or progress I’m telling about ___ supported, or is it a shape I like?

@ numbers | Numbers, data and measurement | number
Where does the figure ___ come from, originally?
Who counted ___, how, and why did they want the number?
What exactly is being counted? What is left out of the count?
Is ___ big or small? Compared with what?
What is ___ per person, per year, per unit, or as a share of the whole?
Is this a count, an estimate, a projection or a guess repeated until it looked like a count?
What is the range or margin of error around ___?
Did the definition or the way of counting change over the period I’m comparing?
Is the change in ___ real, or an artefact of how it was measured?
Am I comparing like with like: same units, same years, same population, same currency, adjusted for inflation?
What would the trend look like if I started the chart at a different year?
Is the average hiding a wide spread? What do the median and the extremes say?
Is there an outlier driving ___? What happens without it?
Could the pattern in ___ be chance?
Who is missing from the data, and how would including them change it?
Does the number pass a sanity check: is it physically, economically, logistically possible?
Do two sources give different figures for ___? Why?
What number would surprise me? What number would contradict my argument?
Does the inventory (what exists) tell the story, or does the activity (what is used, and how often)?
Is this the right measure for my question, or just the one that was available?

@ comparison | Comparison, analogy and context | term event person
What is ___ like? Where does the comparison break down?
Has ___ happened before? What happened then?
What are the closest cases to ___, and how did they turn out differently?
How does ___ compare with ___ on the measure that matters most?
Is ___ typical of its time and place, or an exception?
What did contemporaries compare ___ to? What does that tell me about them?
Am I using an analogy to explain, or to persuade? Would it survive a sceptic?
What does the analogy to ___ hide?
What is the right comparison group for ___: neighbours, rivals, peers, predecessors?
What larger story is ___ a part of?
What smaller story, close to the ground, would show ___ best?
What was going on in the economy, the law, the technology and the culture around ___?
How would ___ look in another country’s history books?
Is my case the most dramatic one rather than the most representative? Does that matter?

@ counterfactual | Counterfactuals and contingency | event person
What if ___ had not happened?
What if ___ had decided the other way?
What was the smallest change that would have produced a different outcome?
Which outcomes were genuinely possible at the time, and which only look possible now?
Was ___ inevitable, likely, or a close call?
Where was the point of no return?
If ___ had been removed from the story, would someone else have done the same?
What did people at the time expect to happen? Why were they wrong, or right?
Which near-miss is the story forgetting?
Does my counterfactual change one thing, or quietly change several?

@ intel | Intelligence analysis: hypotheses, indicators, deception, confidence | claim event person
What are all the plausible hypotheses for ___, including the ones I don’t like?
Which evidence is consistent with every hypothesis, and so tells me nothing?
Which single piece of evidence most clearly separates the hypotheses?
Which hypothesis has the least evidence against it, rather than the most for it?
What would ___ be doing now if hypothesis A were true? If hypothesis B were?
What is the key assumption under my leading hypothesis, and how could I test it?
What would I see first if ___ were about to happen?
Which indicators of ___ are present, which are absent, and which can’t I see?
What would change my assessment of ___ tomorrow?
What is the signal, and what is the noise, in ___?
What has ___ done in the past in similar circumstances?
What are ___’s capabilities, and what are its intentions? Am I confusing the two?
Could ___ be deliberately misleading? Who would benefit if I believed it?
Does ___ have the motive, the opportunity and the means to deceive?
Is the evidence for ___ too convenient, too complete, too timely?
What is ___ hiding, and what are they showing me so I look away?
Has a source that was reliable before become a channel for someone else?
How likely is ___: remote, unlikely, roughly even, likely, very likely, almost certain?
How confident am I in the judgment, as distinct from how likely the event is?
What would move my confidence on ___ up or down?
Am I using “may”, “could” or “possibly” to avoid committing to a judgment?
What is the bottom line in one sentence, for a reader with one minute?
What don’t we know about ___, and does it matter to the judgment?
If this assessment is wrong, how is it most likely to be wrong?
What would a red team say about ___?
What would I tell a decision-maker to watch for next?

@ journalism | Journalism and investigation: money, power, people | person claim event
Who benefits from ___? Who pays?
Where did the money for ___ come from, and where did it go?
Who owns ___, on paper and in fact?
Who signed off on ___? Whose name is on the document?
Who has the power to stop ___, and why haven’t they?
What rules, laws or contracts govern ___? Were they followed?
Who is ___ connected to: family, business partners, former employers, donors?
What documents must exist if ___ is true? Who holds them?
What public records cover ___: courts, company registries, property, procurement, shipping, flight logs?
What has ___ said before, on the record, that I can compare with now?
What can be seen from open sources: satellite imagery, social media, archived web pages?
What can be confirmed by a second, independent source?
Who else was in the room?
Who has left the organisation, and what would they say now?
Who knows the most about ___ and has the least reason to lie?
Who would be hurt if this were published? Is that harm justified?
Have I put every serious allegation to the person it concerns, with time to reply?
What is the strongest case ___ would make for themselves?
Is my source’s account first-hand? What could they not have seen?
What is new here, and what has already been reported?
Why now? Why is this the moment the story matters?
What is the one fact that, if wrong, kills the story?
What is the human story inside the documents?
What will the response be, and is it already answered in the piece?
What would a libel lawyer underline?

@ argument | Argument and the shape of the piece | none claim
What is my argument, in one sentence a stranger could repeat?
What is the question this piece answers? Does the first page ask it?
What does this chapter add that the last one didn’t?
Which section could I cut without the reader noticing?
Where does the argument depend on a step I haven’t shown?
What is the strongest objection, and where do I answer it?
Have I answered the objection, or just mentioned it?
Is ___ evidence, illustration or decoration?
Am I telling the story in the order it happened, or the order that explains it best? Which does this piece need?
Where would a reader stop and ask “how do you know?”
Where would a reader stop and ask “so what?”
What is the best scene, quote or detail I have, and is it in the right place?
Is the ending a conclusion, or a summary?
What do I claim in the introduction that the body never delivers?
What did I learn while writing that the opening doesn’t know yet?
Which claims are mine, and which am I borrowing? Is that clear to the reader?
How certain does the prose sound, and is that how certain I am?
If I had to defend this piece in a room of experts, which page would I dread?

@ reader | The reader | none term
Who is this for, exactly? What do they already know?
What does my reader believe about ___ before they start?
What does my reader need to know first, to follow ___?
Where will my reader get lost? Where will they get bored?
What will my reader remember a week later?
What question will my reader ask that I haven’t answered?
Would a specialist find this too simple, or a newcomer too dense? Which reader matters more here?
What term, acronym or name is my reader meeting for the first time on this page?
What would my reader do differently after reading this?
If my reader only reads the first paragraph, what do they take away?
If my reader is sceptical of my politics, my discipline or me, where do I lose them?
What does my reader care about that I’ve been ignoring?

@ yourself | Yourself: bias, motive and blind spots | none claim person
What did I believe about ___ before I started? Has it changed?
What do I want to be true about ___? Why?
Which evidence did I accept quickly because it agreed with me?
Which evidence did I scrutinise hardest? Would I scrutinise the other side’s as hard?
Am I anchored on the first number, source or story I found?
Am I seeing a pattern because it is there, or because I’ve been looking for it for months?
What would I say about ___ if a rival had written it?
Who do I admire in this story, and has that softened my judgment?
Who do I dislike, and has that sharpened it unfairly?
What part of this research am I avoiding? Why?
Am I writing for the evidence or for an audience I want to please?
What have I been certain of in the past that turned out wrong? Does this feel the same?
What would I need to see to admit I’m wrong about ___?
Am I stuck because the evidence is unclear, or because I don’t like what it says?
What do I know about ___ only from people who agree with each other?
Is my expertise helping here, or making me miss something a beginner would see?

@ question | Questions about the question | none claim
Is ___ the right question, or the one that was easiest to ask?
What would I need to know to answer it? Can that be known at all?
Is this question too big? What smaller question sits inside it?
Is this question too small? What larger question is it part of?
Why does this question matter, and to whom?
Who else has asked it, and what did they conclude?
What question are my sources answering? Is it the same as mine?
Is my question built on a premise I haven’t checked?
Is this a question of fact, of interpretation, of value, or of prediction?
What answer am I expecting? What answer would surprise me?
Is there a better question I’m avoiding because the answer might be uncomfortable?
If I answered this perfectly, what would I do with the answer?
What would a historian, an economist, a soldier or a lawyer ask instead?

@ stuck | When you are stuck | none term person
What do I know for certain about ___? Write it down, even if it’s short.
What is the very next thing I could find out in under an hour?
Which source have I been meaning to read and haven’t?
Who would know? Who could I write to today?
What would I search for if I’d never heard of ___ before?
What other names, spellings or translations might ___ appear under?
Which archive, database or library haven’t I tried?
What did the footnotes of my best secondary source point to that I haven’t followed?
What would I do if I had to file something on ___ tomorrow?
Can I work on a different part of the piece and come back to this?
Am I stuck on the research, or on the writing? They need different fixes.
What would I tell a student who brought me this problem?
If I explain ___ out loud to an empty room, where do I trip?
What if the gap is the finding: what does it mean that ___ can’t be found?
Is it time to narrow the question, or to stop and write what I have?
What is the smallest true thing I can say about ___ right now?
