Revising is where a draft becomes a piece of writing. Anabasis helps in three ways: the **revision lens** shades the sentences worth a second look, the **Revision** view on the Research desk shows every chapter side by side with a checklist for each, and **editing marks** let an editor (or you, on a later pass) mark changes the way a copyeditor does, for the author to accept or reject. Everything runs in the page; nothing is sent anywhere.

## The revision lens

Turn it on with the **Revision lens** button on the toolbar. A bar of chips across the page counts each kind of finding and shades it in the text:

- **Long sentences**: over 35 words. Split them, or make sure each one earns its length.
- **Passive voice**: who did it? Keep the passive when the actor doesn't matter.
- **Hedges and filler**: *very*, *quite*, *arguably*, *it should be noted*.
- **Repeated words**: the same word again within 40 words.
- **Clichés**: *at the end of the day*, *tip of the iceberg*, *a perfect storm* and about ninety more.
- **Same-length runs**: four or more sentences in a row of about the same length, which read as a drone.
- **Pet words**: words this document leans on far more than most writing does.
- **-ly adverbs**: often propping up a weak verb.
- **Buried verbs**: *the implementation of* hides *implement*.

Click a chip to show or hide that kind. The last four start hidden, because they're for a closer pass. The bar also shows the grade level.

## Revision, chapter by chapter

**Research desk → Revision** puts every chapter in one table: words, average sentence length and how much it varies, long sentences, passive voice and hedges per thousand words, clichés, adverbs, buried verbs and a grade level. A shaded number is worth a look, not a rule: a chapter of dialogue will have short, even sentences, and a methods section will have more passive voice.

Choose a row to see that chapter's **revision checklist**:

- The argument is clear by the end of the first page
- Each paragraph does one job, and its first sentence says what
- Transitions carry the reader from paragraph to paragraph
- Every claim of fact has a source, and the sources can bear it
- Quotations are introduced, accurate and explained
- Cut what doesn't serve the argument (aim for a tenth)
- Went through the revision lens
- Names, dates, numbers and spellings checked
- Read it aloud, start to finish

Each item you tick is dated and kept with the project, and the bar in the table fills as you go. **Open with the lens** opens the chapter with the lens on; **CSV** takes the table to a spreadsheet.

## Editing marks

Select some words and press **⌘/Ctrl Alt M** (or **Mark → Editing mark…**). The marks are the copyeditor's set:

| Mark | What it asks for |
|---|---|
| AQ | A query to the author: a question to answer |
| ck | Verify a fact, spelling or quotation against the source |
| stet | Let it stand: keep the original wording |
| del | Take these words out |
| ^ | Replace with other words |
| tr | Transpose: swap the first and last words, or two letters |
| ⁀ | Close up: take out the space |
| # | Add a space |
| cap / lc | Capitalize, or set in lowercase |
| ital / rom / bf | Italic, roman (take italics off), bold |
| sp | Spell out a number or abbreviation |
| ¶ | Start a new paragraph here |
| run in | Join this paragraph to the one before |

Each mark is signed with the name in **Settings** and dated, shows a small tag in the text, and is listed in the **Outline** tab under *Editing marks*.

### Taking the marks

Click a mark in the text, or in the Outline list, to see what it asks. **Accept** makes the change: the words are deleted, transposed, capitalized, spelled out (*7* becomes *seven*) or replaced. **Reject** takes the mark away and leaves the words. For a query, write your answer and choose **Answer and resolve**; the question and answer are kept as a margin comment.

**Make every change** (in the Outline tab) does every mark that can be done automatically, with Undo, and leaves queries, checks and stets for you.

Marks are working markup: Word, PDF, web, e-book and Markdown exports leave them out, with the words as they were.

### Marks from a reviewer

The page made by **Export → For a reviewer…** has an **Editing mark…** menu too, so an editor with nothing but a browser can mark the draft. When you import their review, the marks come in as editing marks under their name, alongside their comments and suggested changes. See [Exporting](Exporting).

## Tips

- **Revise in passes.** One pass for the argument and structure, with the checklist; one for sentences, with the lens; one for the copyedit, with marks.
- **Mark your own draft.** Editing marks work as notes to yourself on a read-through, to act on later without breaking the flow.
- **Read it aloud.** [Listening to the draft](Checking-the-Draft) catches what the eye skips.
